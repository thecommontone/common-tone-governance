## Concepts Demonstrated

### 1. Commercial Peak of the Kendrick-Drake Feud → [[01.1 Pop Music, huh?]]

"Not Like Us" was released May 4, 2024 as the culminating diss track in Kendrick Lamar's ongoing feud with Drake (the feud had intensified across multiple tracks from both artists throughout spring 2024: "Push Ups," "Taylor Made Freestyle," "Euphoria," "6:16 in LA," "Family Matters," "Meet the Grahams," "Not Like Us"). The track reached #1 on the Billboard Hot 100 its first week — a rare diss-track commercial achievement.

Hip-hop diss tracks have recurring cultural presence but rarely achieve mainstream #1 chart success. "Not Like Us" represented a specific 2024 moment where hip-hop internal conflict became mainstream commercial phenomenon. The track subsequently won 2025 Grammy Record of the Year (announced February 2025) and Kendrick performed it at Super Bowl LIX halftime (February 2025). Teaching "Not Like Us" illuminates how specific hip-hop cultural events can achieve extraordinary commercial and cultural reach.

### 2. Diss-Track as Commercial Category → [[01.1 Pop Music, huh?]]

Hip-hop diss tracks have specific genre conventions: direct attacks on named rivals, specific accusations (often escalating through the feud), rhythmic-lyrical virtuosity as demonstration of superiority, and reliance on the target's inability to successfully counter. "Not Like Us" includes all genre conventions while achieving mainstream commercial reach that earlier diss tracks typically didn't.

Earlier commercially-successful diss tracks: Nas vs. Jay-Z (2001-2002), LL Cool J vs. Kool Moe Dee, 50 Cent vs. various. Each operated within hip-hop audiences but rarely achieved mainstream chart dominance. "Not Like Us"'s 2024 commercial success reflected both specific cultural-moment conditions (Drake's polarizing public position, TikTok viral amplification, extensive hip-hop-media coverage) and the track's own craft quality. Teaching this illuminates the diss-track tradition and its evolution.

### 3. Mustard Production → [[02.1 Texture]]

The track was produced by Mustard (Dijon McFarlane), the West Coast hip-hop producer known for his distinctive minimalist, bass-heavy aesthetic. Mustard's production style — sparse percussion, prominent bass, minimal melodic content, space for the rapper's voice — has been central to West Coast hip-hop for a decade (YG's work, various Drake tracks, Travis Scott collaborations).

Mustard's specific contribution on "Not Like Us" is the distinctive piano riff (a pitched-up sample) that opens and recurs throughout. The riff is simple but memorable, providing just enough melodic content to anchor the track while leaving space for Lamar's vocal to dominate. Teaching this production illuminates how hip-hop production craft serves the rapper's lyrical focus rather than competing with it.

### 4. The Specific Accusations → [[01.1 Pop Music, huh?]]

Worth noting honestly: the track contains specific serious accusations against Drake, including allegations of inappropriate conduct with minors. The accusations generated substantial public discussion throughout 2024-2025. Drake subsequently filed a defamation lawsuit against Universal Music Group (the label distributing "Not Like Us") in early 2025 — an unusual legal response to diss-track content that had traditionally been handled via counter-diss tracks.

Teaching "Not Like Us" responsibly means engaging with these accusations directly rather than treating the song as purely musical craft. The song's commercial and cultural impact is inseparable from its specific content about Drake. The ongoing legal proceedings also illuminate contemporary issues around defamation law applied to hip-hop lyric content — a legal territory that will continue developing.

### 5. Minor-Key West-Coast Hip-Hop → [[03.4 Modal Mixture]]

The track is in F# minor, following the minor-key hip-hop template we've tracked throughout the 2000s-2020s. West-Coast hip-hop specifically has a long minor-key tradition (Dr. Dre's *Chronic* era, Ice Cube's work, Kendrick's earlier catalog). "Not Like Us" extends this tradition while deploying Mustard's specific contemporary production aesthetic.

Lamar's career trajectory is worth teaching. He had achieved critical dominance earlier (*To Pimp a Butterfly* 2015, *DAMN.* 2017 — the first non-classical/jazz work to win the Pulitzer Prize for Music), commercial major-label status (*Mr. Morale & the Big Steppers* 2022), and now peak commercial-cultural convergence (2024 Super Bowl halftime, 2025 Grammy Record of the Year). His career represents specific late-2010s-into-2020s hip-hop artistic-commercial peak trajectory.

# Directive Library

Gate-1 proposals draw from this library and adapt to the corpus. Each entry
keeps the proven prompt shape: compile exhaustively · quote exactly · per-item
citation · separate standard facts from opinions from claimed coinages · state
the vault surface it feeds. Chapter references are examples from the 12tone
program — a proposal reads `01_SPECS/CHAPTER_MAP.md` at run time and cites
current IDs.

## L01 — Cadences, endings, degrees of closure ⭐
Everything on how pieces END and how cadences work: authentic vs plagal motion,
deceptive resolutions, avoided cadences, fadeouts, abrupt endings, any judgment
of how *final* a gesture feels. Separately: every instance where non-dominant
motion is treated as a genuine arrival vs a weak substitute — and the opposite.
*Feeds: Graded Closure + Plagal Paradigm evidence.*

## L02 — The IV chord and the subdominant's power ⭐
Every discussion of IV, subdominant function, ♭VII, plagal and double-plagal
motion, "Amen" gestures; structural vs decorative; claims that rock/pop harmony
runs by different rules than classical. *Feeds: Plagal Paradigm evidence.*

## L03 — The small devices ⭐⭐ (Devices Cabinet)
Every compact named harmonic trick: backdoor progressions, common-tone
diminished 7ths, line clichés, Picardy thirds, gear-change key changes,
secondary leading-tone chords, borrowed iv, chromatic passing chords, pedal
points, sus resolutions, and the rest. Per device: definition in the creator's
words, every example, when/why it works, whether the name is standard or the
creator's own. *Historically the highest-yield directive.*

## L04 — Repeated approaches, turnarounds, delayed arrivals
Turnarounds, repeated cadential approaches, ii–V motion, delayed resolutions,
setups postponed or repeated before delivery, two-chord vamps; the claimed
EFFECT of repetition/delay on the listener. *Feeds: Stuttering ii-V neighborhood.*

## L05 — Sections, transitions, the moments BETWEEN sections ⭐
Form functions and especially TRANSITIONS: fills, build-ups, drops, risers,
pre-chorus behavior, the bar before a chorus lands, any gesture that ANNOUNCES
a coming section. Separately: unusual/through-composed forms; drums/production
articulating structure. *Feeds: The Herald evidence.*

## L06 — Texture, arrangement, layers
How parts are layered and what each layer's job is; bass lines that leave the
chords; melodies floating free of harmony; doubling and role-splitting;
production choices as structure; groove/pocket/interlock. *Feeds: Stream
Leading evidence. Watch for relayed scholarly frameworks (Moore, Lavengood,
Peres…) — credit them, never the creator.*

## L07 — Blues inheritance
Blue notes, ♭3/♭7 over major harmony, blues-scale use, 12-bar mutations, claims
about blues practice becoming standard pop vocabulary. *Feeds: the
blues-naturalization thesis — record confirmations AND complications.*

## L08 — Modes as living vocabulary
Modal songs and borrowings; the ♭7 in major keys; the ♯4; whether modal traits
are treated as native pop language or exotic deviations — quote the evaluative
language. Include contested/ambiguous keys. *Feeds: "major is not just Ionian".*

## L09 — Tonal ambiguity and the missing tonic
Every case of ambiguous, contested, absent, or implied-but-never-played tonics;
competing hearings and the creator's verdict, quoted. Note where the creator
DECLINES an ambiguity reading. *Feeds: the Imaginary/Hypothetical Tonic.*

## L10 — Motives and melodic construction
The creator's motivic METHOD as a method (identification, transformation
tracking, unity claims), then the best analyses. Label the method a claimed
signature only where it demonstrably differs from textbook practice; name the
sourced scholars otherwise. *Watch: counterfactual-variation moves.*

## L11 — Coinages, hot takes, running arguments (credit ledger)
Every original-seeming term, named concept, in-joke, or framework: definition,
first appearance, examples — split HIS / RELAYED / UNCLEAR, with contested
cases listed for Doug. Separately: strongest opinions, orthodoxy attacks,
public mind-changes, named running arguments. *Crediting decisions are Doug's
alone; the ledger only assembles evidence.*

## L12 — The full item/song-analysis index
One dense line per item receiving substantial analysis: work — creator's item
(id, date, title) — main theoretical points. A routing index: completeness
over depth. *Feeds: REPERTOIRE enrichment (enrichment input only — never
auto-create CTSF entries).*

## L13 — Genre-mechanics essays *(adopted by Doug's ruling, 2026-08-28)*
Episodes dissecting a genre's mechanical signature: half-time/double-time
grooves, breakdown conventions, groove-as-genre-marker, production gestures a
style is built on. Distinct from L06 (texture/layers) in that the subject is
the GENRE's convention, not one arrangement. *Feeds: rhythm/meter and groove
chapters (e.g. `01.6`, `04.7`) + BEATS & GROOVES. Provenance: surfaced by the
twelvetone-local dry-run scan (39 supporting items; top evidence in that
program's SAMPLE_EVIDENCE.md).*

## L14 — Music-history & pedagogy-lore essays *(adopted by Doug's ruling, 2026-08-28)*
Historical and lore-of-the-discipline essays: origins of notation and
solmization, contested histories, myths the field tells about itself. **Routing
(Doug's ruling): extractions land in `00_WORKSHOP/1-Drafting/` for Doug to
place by hand** — they feed Medieval Whispers boxes and cultural sidebars, not
theory folders. *Provenance: twelvetone-local dry-run scan (10 supporting
items).*
